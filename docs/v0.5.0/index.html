∴ Station 1 · the Clay Pit

Stir the clay. Nothing knows north.

Wet clay carries tiny iron-oxide grains, magnetite and hematite, each a little compass. In the pit they tumble at random: stir them and they only swirl. Their arrows cancel out.

Drag or move across the clay. Grains are drawn huge and few; this is a picture of the idea, model, not a simulation of real clay.

≡ Side path · the Tablet lore

Wedges pressed into clay.

Under the pit, a tablet. People pressed wedges into wet clay long before anyone fired a brick to remember north; the site's tally already counts in Uruk-style wedges.

These marks are decorative, drawn for this site. They are not a real inscription and not a translation.

△ Station 2 · the Kiln

Fire it. 1050 °C.

In the kiln the clay becomes ceramic. Far above the grains' ordering temperatures, every grain's moment flips freely; any old direction is wiped.

1050 °C is the firing peak this site already uses (the Ward quest), shown as an illustration, not a measured firing curve for any brick.

↟ Station 3 · the Cooling

Let it cool. Then it remembers.

As the brick cools, grains stop flipping: hematite below its Néel temperature ≈675 °C, magnetite below its Curie temperature ≈580 °C1,2. They freeze in a slight preference for Earth's field at that moment: the north that was.

◆ 675 °C hematite ◆ 580 °C magnetite

Drag the compass onto the brick.

Alignment exaggerated for the eye. Real thermoremanence is a small statistical bias, and the direction here is illustrative, not a measured declination.

◷ Station 5 · the Long Years lore

Seasons. Then centuries.

Rain, frost, lichen, sun. The face weathers. The arrows inside are drawn unchanged, which is the hope, not a result: how weathering affects a brick's remanence is not measured here.

Redcore · Brickmark · v0.5.0 · Room I · the Hearth

The brick remembers
where north was.

As fired clay cools, its magnetite locks a record of Earth's field (magnetite Curie temperature ≈580 °C; hematite Néel temperature ≈675 °C1,2). Brickmark asks whether that record, plus the clay's own texture, can tell one batch, and maybe one course, from another.

Your quest: lay a course of ten bricks with a phone and twenty clay faces, then deliver it to the assay. Tinder, the kiln sprite, will walk you there.

    Room V · Spectrum Gate #3 · UNMEASURED
    
    
    The gate is a low-pass filter. Turn the dial.
    
    
    Model: grain-scale magnetization is roughly uncorrelated, so the field at standoff h is that source upward-continued, multiplied in the Fourier domain by exp(−2πkh). Standoff is the low-pass filter; cutoff wavelength ≈ 2πh.
    
    
    
    The gate: on one strong face, scan at 0.25–0.5 mm pitch, repeat at 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 5 mm standoff, and compare measured B-maps' height attenuation against exp(−2πkh). If the measured spectrum is much redder, the unit assay may be dead.
    
    
    Controls: empty-jig scan, same scan twice without remount, scan after remount, magnetic-sheet control. A phone photo is not a B-map and does not touch this gate. Issue #3 →
