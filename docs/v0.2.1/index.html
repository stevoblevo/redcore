The brick remembers
where north was.

As fired clay cools in the kiln, its magnetite locks a record of Earth's field (magnetite Curie temperature ≈580 °C; hematite Néel temperature ≈675 °C1,2). Brickmark asks whether that record, plus the clay's own texture, can tell one batch, and maybe one course, from another. Help us find out with a phone and twenty bricks.

Read before quoting numbers

The v4 figures — 87 independent cells and 63 extractable bits per face — are MODEL ONLY outputs of a simulation run, not measured. Do not publish a bits-per-brick number as an empirical claim until the spectrum gate (issue #3) is run.

Chapter 1 · The assay

What Brickmark is

A provenance assay

A magnetic provenance assay for fired clay. Batch provenance first; spatial, unit or course identity only if repeatability survives measurement.

Not a PUF

No PUF-as-crypto, no bed-face archive in a laid wall, no security language until repeatability is proven. The struck v1 claims stay dead.

The course is the unit

The identity unit may be the course of bricks, not a single brick: aggregate four to eight bricks for a usable identity length, if measured course BER earns it.

Spectrum gate #3
UNMEASURED

    
Model: grain-scale magnetization is roughly uncorrelated, so the field at standoff h is that source upward-continued, multiplied in the Fourier domain by exp(−2πkh). Standoff is the low-pass filter; cutoff wavelength ≈ 2πh.

    
The gate: on one strong face, scan at 0.25–0.5 mm pitch, repeat at 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 5 mm standoff, and compare measured B-maps' height attenuation against exp(−2πkh). If the measured spectrum is much redder, the unit assay may be dead.

    
    
Controls: empty-jig scan, same scan twice without remount, scan after remount, magnetic-sheet control. A phone photo is not a B-map and does not touch this gate. Issue #3 →
